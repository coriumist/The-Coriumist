The Money Map

We track the money's movement. Never the people.

Why this exists

Capital leaves a wake in the sky and on the water. Count the flights between seven airports near Davos in January and you have the attendance sheet of the World Economic Forum. Count the yachts along the Cote d'Azur in late September and you have the guest list of the Monaco Yacht Show, minus the names.

Aggregate enough of it and you can see where the room is going next. That is the entire product. Not who. Where.

01 · The method

Three rules.

Aggregate

We count flows between airports and ports. Never an individual aircraft. Never a hull. The pattern is the product; the tail number is none of our business.

Delayed

Patterns over weeks and seasons. Never a live pin. Anything that could locate a person today does not belong on this page.

Anonymous

No names, no tail numbers, no vessel identities, no claims about who was aboard. The room, never the guest list.

Patterns over time, never a live pin.

04 · The thesis

Wealth is a where.

Ask why the same billionaires, chief executives, and technology founders keep ending up at the same dinners, the same harbors, the same hotel bars, and the answers come back in layers. Some of it is business. Some of it is obligation. Some of it is status maintenance, the quiet tax on remaining legible to the people who matter. Some of it is pleasure, because a week on the water in September is genuinely pleasant.

Under all of it is something simpler: proximity. Put yourself in the room and luck has a chance to mix with opportunity. The chance encounter at the hotel bar. The seat next to the billionaire at the fish restaurant in Cape Cod. The pitch that only happens because two people were standing on the same dock at the same time. None of it is plannable. All of it is positionable.

The map is where the money moves. The rooms are where it sits down to eat. Under both is the human story: everyone in the room is there because being somewhere else felt like missing something.

05 · The cost

Two kinds of passengers.

The circuit carries two kinds of people. The first owns the yacht. The second works in business development for a major firm and is maintaining the appearance of belonging: the right hotel, the right dinners, the right harbor at the right week, expensed and carefully documented, because access is the job.

From the outside the two look identical. From the inside the difference is everything. One of them is on vacation. The other is performing proximity, week after week, season after season, in Sardinia in yacht week and in Davos in January, smiling through the fourth dinner of the night with people who might one day take a meeting.

Sometimes the performance pays. A friendship forms. A door opens. That is the entire economics of the circuit: expensive, exhausting, and occasionally, for someone standing in exactly the right room, worth it.

This page maps the rooms. It does not pretend everyone in them is having fun.

07 · How it is built

Public inputs only.

Version one of the Money Map is built on published reports: Greenpeace's "Davos in the Sky," airport authority traffic figures, organizer announcements, and the trade press. Every number on this page carries its year and its source.

What comes next: live ADS-B and AIS feeds, airport-pair flow counts computed weekly, and a corridor index for every room on the circuit.

What will never be built: a live pin on a person. The method forbids it and so do we.

The Coriumist Index is an intelligence visualization tool. This is not financial advice or investment guidance.
